How a volume-driven testing culture feeds the loop

Section 03 · Volume, Value, and Testing

Volume-driven testing is not neutral. It feeds itself, and every turn leaves quality a little worse.

Progress is measured by volumeTest cases, coverage, scripts
Testing starts after the buildNo time for questions before code
Defects are found lateWhen they cost the most to fix
Rework and firefightingThe sprint goes to fixing, not thinking
Upfront thinking is cut firstRisk analysis waits for a free afternoon
More pressure to show progressSo we count more tests
Degraded qualityEach turn adds more tests and less information
A reinforcing loop

Nothing in this loop asks whether the testing produced information anyone could act on. Every turn adds volume and removes time for thinking.

AI on the volume side

Point AI at writing more test cases, and the same loop simply turns faster.

What breaks the loop

Information that arrives while a decision is still cheap to change.

Upfront rigour before the build.

Not more tests after it.

Agentic QCSD is built on Designed Agency

Section 06 · Designed Agency

The industry adopted the word agentic faster than it examined what the word means. Designed Agency compels us to think about and define what it means in our context.

Why most AI agents are called agentic
  • An LLM decides the next step
  • It calls tools
  • It runs in a loop
What that leaves out
  • Encoding judgment without architecture is just a prompt
  • Architecture without encoded judgment is just plumbing
  • Without honest approximation there is no way of knowing whether what was built is any good
Designed Agency

Judgment deliberately designed in by practitioners who know the work deeply enough to encode it, architect it and measure it honestly.

  • Encoded the threads, the material the cloth is made of
  • Structured the loom, the instrument that acts on the material
  • Approximated the contact between threads that gives the cloth its texture
The cloth needs all three together. Agentic QCSD and the Agentic QE framework are built on all three.

PACTS: five interrogation tools

Apply PACTS · 15:20–15:55

Each property is a question, not a definition. Walk it against your reports, score each report 0 to 3 per property in pairs, and use the probe when the answer is thin.

P

Proactive

Did it flag risk before being asked?

“Cite the exact src/ lines that drove your conclusion.”
A

Autonomous

Did it decide what to inspect for itself?

“What is the most important thing you did not look at?”
C

Collaborative

Did the AI agents reference each other?

“Do AI agents A and B agree on the top three risks?”
T

Targeted

Was it fitted to this checkout code?

“Rewrite this for our context, not a generic checklist.”
S

Structured

Can you trace each conclusion to its evidence?

“Show the trail: which AI agent decided this, from which input, and why?”
Compare engines. If your pair has a Claude Code user and someone on another tool, compare the two reports for the same exercise. That gap is the value of the orchestration layer.
